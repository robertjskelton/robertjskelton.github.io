I recently bought a Lotus Elise, and the head unit that came with it was a flip out screen which hit the windshield wiper when it opened. I liked the Power [Acoustik Headunit]((https://www.walmart.com/ip/Power-Acoustik-CP-71WAK-Single-DIN-6-5-Color-Touchscreen-Car-Stereo-with-Bluetooth-Connected-Apple-CarPlay-Android-Auto/17530903908)) I had in my Ferrari 360, and decided to put the newer version of that stereo in. The new version has a volume knob instead of a volume up and down button. 

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/18.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/19.jpg)

I also installed a backup camera, which took the majority of the install time due to running wires to the rear of the car. The Elise has some pretty bad blind spots, so I wanted to upgrade to having a backup camera, Android Auto, and single DIN to not block the air vents.

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/28.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/5.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/4.jpg)

The install of the head unit was easy, it was a [single wiring harness to buy](https://amzn.to/3Ps4MJf), two wires to swap on that harness (swap the red and the yellow on the ISO connector), and it just worked! 

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/17.jpg)

For the [reverse camera]((https://amzn.to/4td4UKT)), I ran the wires under the dash to the center tunnel (you don't have a center console on an Elise), then under the rear carpet, through a firewall behind the driver's seat into the engine bay, through the engine bay into a grommet into the trunk area, and finally into another grommet near the license plate lights. Having a [fish tape](https://amzn.to/4sGs90e) was required and super helpful.

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/6.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/7.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/8.jpg)

I wired the power and ground into the reverse lights, which are near the license plate lights using [2x t-taps](https://amzn.to/4lQYPBq). 

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/9.jpg)

I wired the reverse trigger of the head unit to the reverse line on the wiring harness I bought using a [heat shrink connector](https://amzn.to/4deD8Jw), so it automatically turns the reverse camera on when you shift into reverse.

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/24.jpg)

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/22.jpg)

And of course, had to drill a small hole for the reverse camera.  

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/11.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/12.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/13.jpg)

And finally, I sealed it all up. I used [cloth wiring tape](https://amzn.to/40Y5vEw) on all the wiring that runs through the engine bay for heat prevention. 

I added some [Dynamat](https://amzn.to/3O8d0FT) I had leftover on the firewall and under the passenger seat. I found a floor mat trim button I was missing under the seat too! And a quarter.

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/23.jpg)

I changed the boot screen to have a [matching green Lotus Elise logo](https://www.lotustalk.com/threads/3rd-times-the-charm-my-attempt-at-another-single-din-carplay-radio-power-acoustic-cp71-w-this-one-seems-promising.547053/). 

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/20.jpg)

I put in a new engine [air filter](https://amzn.to/4sC7tX7) since it was easily accessible, and cleaned the wheel liner.

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/10.jpg)

So far, this is a great upgrade and pretty reasonably priced. Head unit was $160, wiring harness $10, backup camera $40, so $210 total. 

![Radio](/images/elise_radio/14.jpg)
![Radio](/images/elise_radio/26.jpg)
